- Complete beginners.
- Students learning computerized accounting.
- Accountants moving from manual books to Tally.
- Small-business owners.
- GST billing operators.

The handbook focuses on both:

1. **Concepts** — why something exists.
2. **Practical usage** — how it is used in a real business.

Tally.ERP 9 is still useful for learning legacy environments and understanding core Tally accounting concepts. Statutory rules such as GST, TDS, TCS, e-invoicing, e-way bills, return formats, thresholds and due dates can change. Always verify current legal requirements before using this handbook for live statutory compliance.

As of this handbook's review on **13 August 2026**, Tally's active product line is TallyPrime, and Tally provides a supported migration path from Tally.ERP 9. # 3. Tally.ERP 9 vs TallyPrime

The fundamental accounting concepts remain similar, but the interface and workflow differ.

| Area | Tally.ERP 9 | TallyPrime |
|---|---|---|
| Main navigation | Gateway of Tally | Go To / modern navigation |
| Interface | Traditional | Redesigned |
| Search | Limited compared with Prime | Stronger global navigation |
| Reporting | Powerful | More streamlined |
| Statutory support | Legacy builds | Current product focus |
| Learning value | Excellent for legacy systems | Better for new deployments |

If you work in an organization that still uses Tally.ERP 9, you should learn the exact menus and shortcuts of ERP 9.

Do not open or migrate the only copy of production data merely to explore the newer product. Take a verified backup, retain the original data, test conversion on a copy, and reconcile company count, voucher count, trial balance, receivables, payables, inventory quantity/value, taxes, bank balances, and critical reports before users switch systems.

# 4. Accounting Foundations You Must Know

Before learning Tally, understand basic accounting.

## 4.1 Assets

Resources owned or controlled by a business.

Examples:

- Cash
- Bank balance
- Furniture
- Machinery
- Vehicles
- Stock
- Debtors
- Computers

## 4.2 Liabilities

Amounts payable by the business.

Examples:

- Bank loan
- Supplier payable
- Outstanding expenses
- Taxes payable

## 4.3 Capital

Owner's investment in the business.

Basic equation:

```text
Assets = Liabilities + Capital
```

## 4.4 Revenue

Income earned from business operations.

Examples:

- Sales
- Service income
- Commission income

## 4.5 Expenses

Costs incurred to operate the business.

Examples:

- Rent
- Salary
- Electricity
- Internet
- Freight
- Advertising

## 4.6 Debit and Credit

Tally is based on double-entry accounting.

A simple traditional rule:

| Account Type | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | Receiver | Giver |
| Real | What comes in | What goes out |
| Nominal | Expenses/Losses | Income/Gains |

Modern understanding:

### Asset

```text
Increase → Debit
Decrease → Credit
```

### Liability

```text
Increase → Credit
Decrease → Debit
```

### Expense

```text
Increase → Debit
```

### Income

```text
Increase → Credit
```

### Capital

```text
Increase → Credit
```

## Example: Cash Sale

Goods sold for ₹10,000 cash.

```text
Cash A/c Dr        10,000
    To Sales A/c            10,000
```

Cash increases → Debit.

Sales income increases → Credit.

## Example: Rent Paid

```text
Rent A/c Dr         5,000
    To Cash A/c              5,000
```

Expense increases → Debit.

Cash decreases → Credit.

## Educational vs Licensed Usage

Tally installations operate differently depending on licensing status. Educational mode is intended for learning and historically restricts voucher entry to particular calendar dates. Tally's own legacy help pages describe different permitted dates for different releases, so check the message shown by the installed build instead of assuming one universal date rule. Some licensed or connected features may also be unavailable.

For training, focus on:

- Navigation.
- Master creation.
- Voucher entry.
- Reports.
- Data backup.

Do not use Educational mode as a live-books workaround. It can force artificial transaction dates and produce misleading aging, tax periods, inventory movement, and bank reconciliation.

Before installation or upgrade:

1. obtain the installer and support files from Tally or an authorized source, not an unverified download site;
2. confirm operating-system and license requirements for the exact release;
3. record the current application release and data path;
4. take a verified company-data backup;
5. keep installer/license information under authorized control;
6. test opening the copied data before changing production.

# 8. Chart of Accounts: Groups and Ledgers

Tally organizes accounts using:

```text
Group
  └── Ledger
```

Example:

```text
Sundry Debtors
  ├── Customer A
  ├── Customer B
  └── Customer C
```

A **group** controls classification and report presentation; a **ledger** stores transaction balances. Tally.ERP 9 includes predefined groups and two predefined ledgers: `Cash` and `Profit & Loss A/c`. Use the existing Cash ledger unless the business genuinely needs additional cash ledgers such as `Petty Cash—Mumbai`.

Grouping errors do not normally break debit-credit equality, but they can move balances to the wrong report. A customer under `Sales Accounts` can make revenue and receivables misleading even though the Trial Balance still balances.

## 8.1 Common Predefined Groups

### Capital Account

Examples:

- Proprietor Capital
- Partner Capital

### Current Assets

Includes:

- Cash
- Bank
- Debtors
- Inventory-related assets

### Current Liabilities

Includes:

- Creditors
- Duties and taxes
- Outstanding expenses

### Sundry Debtors

Customers who owe money to the business.

### Sundry Creditors

Suppliers to whom the business owes money.

### Sales Accounts

Example:

```text
Local Sales
Interstate Sales
Service Sales
Export Sales
```

### Purchase Accounts

Example:

```text
Local Purchase
Interstate Purchase
Raw Material Purchase
```

### Direct Expenses

Expenses closely related to production or purchase.

Examples:

- Freight inward
- Factory wages
- Carriage inward

### Indirect Expenses

Administrative or operating expenses.

Examples:

- Office rent
- Telephone
- Salary
- Advertisement

### Direct Income

Income directly related to the principal activity or trading account presentation in the entity's accounting design. Classification depends on business substance; do not place ordinary sales here merely to make a desired gross profit.

### Indirect Income

Examples:

- Interest received
- Discount received
- Commission received

Interest or commission can be direct in one business and indirect in another. Choose grouping from the entity's business model and reporting policy, not from the account name alone.

## 8.2 Creating a Ledger

Typical path:



# 9. Accounting Vouchers

A voucher records a business transaction.

When saved as a regular accounting voucher, Tally updates the affected ledgers and reports. Inventory-enabled invoice modes can update stock at the same time. A voucher should contain the business date, voucher type/number, party or account, amount, bill or inventory allocation, narration, and tax/cost-centre details required by the setup.

Payment, receipt, and contra screens may appear in single-entry or double-entry mode depending on configuration. The display mode changes data entry, not double-entry accounting: every saved accounting voucher must still produce balanced debit and credit effects.

Core voucher types:

| Voucher | Common Purpose |
|---|---|
| Contra | Cash ↔ Bank transfer |
| Payment | Money paid |
| Receipt | Money received |
| Journal | Adjustments/non-cash entries |
| Sales | Goods/services sold |
| Purchase | Goods/services purchased |
| Credit Note | Sales return/credit adjustment |
| Debit Note | Purchase return/debit adjustment |

# 13. Journal Voucher

Shortcut:

```text
F7
```

Used for adjustment entries and many non-cash transactions.

## Scenario 1: Depreciation

Computer value: ₹100,000.

Depreciation: ₹20,000.

Preferred accumulated-depreciation presentation:

```text
Depreciation A/c Dr            20,000
    To Accumulated Depreciation—Computers A/c  20,000
```

This preserves gross computer cost and separately shows accumulated depreciation. Some businesses credit the asset ledger directly; use one policy consistently and reconcile it to the fixed-asset schedule.

## Scenario 2: Outstanding Salary

Salary for March not yet paid:

```text
Salary A/c Dr       60,000
    To Salary Payable A/c    60,000
```

## Scenario 3: Prepaid Insurance

If ₹12,000 annual insurance contains ₹3,000 relating to next year:

```text
Prepaid Insurance A/c Dr  3,000
    To Insurance Expense A/c 3,000
```

## Scenario 4: Bad Debt

Customer cannot pay ₹8,000.

```text
Bad Debts A/c Dr     8,000
    To Customer A/c           8,000
```

## Item Invoice vs Accounting Invoice

### Item Invoice

Best when:

- Inventory is maintained.
- Quantity and rate are important.
- Stock movement must be updated.

Example:

```text
Laptop Bag
Qty: 10
Rate: ₹800
Amount: ₹8,000
```

### Accounting Invoice

Useful for:

- Services.

# 18. Memorandum and Optional Vouchers

These features are for provisional or scenario reporting; they are not substitutes for recording a real, approved transaction in the regular books.

| Feature | Regular Books Affected? | How It Is Used | Typical Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memorandum voucher | No until converted | Separate memo voucher that can later be converted | Uncertain or incomplete transaction |
| Optional voucher | No while marked optional | A normal voucher type is marked optional | Scenario or provisional version of a familiar transaction |
| Reversing journal | Not posted to regular books; affects reports only when included for its validity period | Special journal expires/reverses after specified date | Short-term interim reporting adjustment |

## Memorandum Voucher

Used for provisional entries that should not immediately affect books permanently.

Example:

A manager gives an employee ₹5,000 for expected travel spending, but the final expense classification is not yet known.

A memorandum can be used temporarily depending on company policy.

When the final facts are known, convert it to an appropriate regular voucher or remove it from the active scenario. Do not forget it merely because it does not affect the ordinary books.

## Optional Voucher

An optional voucher can represent a transaction that should not currently affect regular books.

Possible uses:

- Provisional sales.
- Scenario analysis.
- Expected purchases.
- Temporary estimates.

An optional voucher is not a separate voucher type; a supported regular voucher is marked optional. It must be included in a scenario to affect that scenario's reports.

# 19. Reversing Journal

A reversing journal is useful for temporary period-end adjustments.

Example:

March closing provision:

```text

    To Audit Fees Payable     30,000
```

In Tally.ERP 9, a reversing journal is effective only for the specified period and only in reports/scenarios that include it; it does not become an ordinary posted accrual. If the statutory or regular books require the expense and liability, record an approved regular journal and reverse/settle it through the normal accounting process.

Use case:

- Management accounts.
- Provisional monthly reporting.
- Temporary accruals.

```text
Sales              ₹1,000,000
Cost of Goods Sold   ₹600,000
Gross Profit         ₹400,000

Salary               ₹150,000
Rent                  ₹50,000
Other Expense         ₹20,000

Net Profit            ₹180,000
```

For a trading business, purchases are not automatically equal to COGS:

```text
COGS = Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Costs - Closing Stock
```

Example: opening stock ₹100,000 + purchases ₹650,000 - closing stock ₹150,000 = COGS ₹600,000, ignoring other direct costs. This distinction prevents the common mistake of treating unsold inventory as a current-period expense.

# 55. Trial Balance

Trial Balance shows ledger balances.

Accounting integrity check:

```text
Total Debit = Total Credit
```

However, a balanced trial balance does not guarantee that every entry is correct.

Example:

If office rent is accidentally posted to advertising expense, debit and credit can still balance.

Use the Trial Balance to find unusual balances, drill into ledgers, and compare with the prior period. A difference between total debits and credits signals a data or extraction problem, but equal totals do not detect omitted transactions, duplicates, wrong parties, wrong dates, or equal-value misclassifications.

---

# 56. Cash Flow and Funds Flow

## Cash Flow

Shows movement of cash and cash equivalents.

Typical categories:

- Operating activities.
- Investing activities.
- Financing activities.

## Funds Flow

Traditionally focuses on movement of working capital/funds between periods.

Use them to answer:

```text
Where did money come from?
Where was money used?
```

Cash flow classifies movements as operating, investing, and financing and reconciles opening to closing cash/cash equivalents. Profit is not cash: credit sales, unpaid expenses, depreciation, stock growth, asset purchases, loans, and owner capital create differences.

Funds flow is a traditional working-capital analysis and is not a substitute for the statutory Cash Flow Statement. Use the report format and classification required by the applicable accounting framework.

# 62. TallyVault and Data Security

TallyVault encrypts company data to help preserve confidentiality. The password is critical: Tally's official guidance warns that forgetting it may make the data inaccessible. Store the password under an authorized recovery process and test access to backups; do not put the only copy in one person's memory.

Security should include:

```text
Tally Security
+ Operating System Security
+ Disk Security
+ Backup Security
+ Network Security
+ Physical Security
```

Do not treat application password protection as a complete cybersecurity strategy.

Encryption does not prevent an authorized user from making an incorrect entry, and it does not replace backups, malware protection, device controls, least privilege, or an edit history.

# 63. Backup, Restore and Data Maintenance

## Backup

Backup protects against:

- Disk failure.
- Accidental deletion.
- Corruption.
- Malware.
- User error.

Recommended conceptual backup strategy:

```text
Daily local backup
+ Separate external/network backup
+ Offsite/cloud backup where policy allows
+ Periodic restore test
```

## Restore

A backup is useful only if it can be restored.

Practice restore using a test location.

Do not restore directly over the only live data folder. Restore to a separate destination, open and verify the company, compare key counts/reports, and then use an approved recovery plan. Label backups with company, period, date/time, application release, and encryption requirements.

## Data Verification

Periodically check data integrity where supported.

Verification is especially important before data splitting, migration, major import, or release change. A clean verification result does not replace accounting reconciliation.

Benefits:

- Faster operations.
- Cleaner year-wise archive.

Before splitting:

1. Take verified backup.
2. Complete reconciliation.
3. Resolve errors.
4. Confirm opening balances.
5. Test split data.

Splitting creates period-based child data and is not mandatory merely because a financial year changed. Review open orders, unreconciled bank items, bill references, tax linkages, and operational documents in both resulting datasets before sign-off. Keep the unsplit backup recoverable.

Important controls:

## User Rights

Restrict:

- Print.
- Back-date.

Every important transaction should have evidence.

```text

```

```text

When?
Why?
Which document supports it?

```

Tally.ERP 9 includes a legacy Tally Audit feature that can help review selected changes, but it should not be assumed to satisfy every current legal edit-log requirement. Confirm the applicable company-law requirement, retention period, enabled product, and auditor expectations before relying on a legacy audit listing.

A review report is useful only if ordinary users cannot bypass it by sharing administrator credentials, changing system dates, deleting evidence, or restoring older data without control. Protect backups and administration with the same care as live vouchers.

# 80. Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1. What is Tally.ERP 9?

Tally.ERP 9 is business management software used for accounting, inventory, taxation, payroll, banking and reporting.

## Q2. What is a ledger?

A ledger is an individual account used to record financial transactions, such as Cash, Rent, Customer A or Supplier B.

## Q3. What is a group?

A group classifies ledgers for reporting.

Example:

```text
Sundry Debtors
  └── Customer A
```

## Q4. Difference between Debtor and Creditor?

Debtor:

```text
Customer owes money to business.
```

Creditor:

```text
Business owes money to supplier.
```

## Q5. What is a Contra Voucher?

Used mainly for cash-bank and bank-bank transfers.

## Q6. Payment vs Contra?

Payment:

```text
Money paid as expense or liability settlement.
```

Contra:

```text
Transfer between cash and bank accounts.
```

## Q7. What is a Journal Voucher?

Used for accounting adjustments, accruals, depreciation and non-cash entries.

## Q8. What is bill-wise tracking?

Tracking receivables/payables invoice by invoice.

## Q9. What is a cost centre?

A dimension used to allocate income or expense by department, project, employee, branch or activity.

## Q10. What is a godown?

A stock location or warehouse.

## Q11. Difference between stock group and stock category?

Stock group provides primary hierarchical classification. Stock category provides an additional parallel classification where enabled.

## Q12. What is a Stock Journal?

Used for stock transfer, consumption, production and internal inventory movement.

## Q13. What is a Receipt Note?

Records goods received before or independently of the purchase invoice workflow.

## Q14. What is a Delivery Note?

Records goods dispatched before or independently of the sales invoice workflow.

## Q15. What is GST?

Goods and Services Tax, an indirect tax framework applied to taxable supplies under Indian law.

## Q16. CGST + SGST vs IGST?

Simplified:

```text
Intrastate → CGST + SGST
Interstate → IGST
```

Subject to place-of-supply rules.

## Q17. What is TDS?

Tax Deducted at Source.

## Q18. What is TCS?

Tax Collected at Source.

## Q19. What is bank reconciliation?

Matching book bank transactions with the bank statement and explaining timing/errors.

## Q20. What is Trial Balance?

A report of ledger balances used to verify debit-credit equality and review accounts.

## Q21. Does a balanced trial balance prove all entries are correct?

No. Misclassification and some equal-value errors can still exist.

## Q22. What is negative stock?

Stock quantity below zero, often caused by sales/issues exceeding recorded available stock.

## Q23. What is an optional voucher?

A voucher that does not currently affect regular books and can be used for provisional/scenario purposes.

## Q24. What is a reversing journal?

A temporary adjustment configured to reverse after a specified period.

## Q25. Why are backups important?

They protect accounting data from hardware failure, corruption, accidental deletion and other loss.

# Appendix F — Multi-Currency Accounting

Tally.ERP 9 can be used in businesses that transact in more than one currency.

Examples:

```text
Base Currency: INR
Foreign Currencies:
USD
EUR
GBP
AED
```

## Core Concepts

### Base Currency

The company's primary reporting currency.

Example:

```text
Indian company → INR
```

### Foreign Currency

Currency used for a specific customer, supplier, bank or transaction.

### Exchange Rate

The conversion relationship between two currencies.

Illustrative example:

```text
1 USD = ₹84
```

Invoice:

```text
USD 1,000
```

Book value:

```text
₹84,000
```

If the customer pays when:

```text
1 USD = ₹85
```

Receipt value:

```text
₹85,000
```

Difference:

```text
₹1,000 exchange gain
```

Depending on accounting rules and configuration, exchange differences are recognized appropriately.

## Practical Scenario

A software company invoices a US customer:

```text
Invoice: USD 5,000
Rate on invoice date: ₹84
INR equivalent: ₹420,000
```

Payment date rate:

```text
₹85
```

INR received:

```text
₹425,000
```

Potential exchange gain:

```text
₹5,000
```

## Learning Points

- Create required currency masters.
- Understand base vs foreign currency.
- Enter exchange rates carefully.
- Revalue foreign balances where accounting policy requires.
- Review realized vs unrealized exchange differences.
- Do not manually force an exchange difference merely to make a ledger match.

# Appendix H — Alter, Display, Delete and Cancel

Tally users must understand the difference between viewing and changing data.

## Display

View data without intending to modify it.

## Alter

Open existing master or voucher for modification.

## Delete

Remove a voucher/master where permitted.

Deleting accounting data should be tightly controlled.

## Cancel

A cancelled voucher can preserve the voucher number/reference while nullifying its accounting effect, depending on workflow and configuration.

### Why Cancellation Can Be Better Than Deletion

Suppose invoice:


Deleting it may create a numbering gap with no explanation.

A controlled cancellation process may preserve:


This improves auditability.

Use company policy and applicable statutory requirements.

# Appendix P — Inventory Valuation Methods

Closing stock value affects profit.

Common valuation concepts include:

- FIFO.
- Average Cost.
- Last Purchase Cost.
- Standard Cost.
- Other supported valuation approaches depending on configuration.

## FIFO Example

Purchases:

```text
10 units @ ₹100
10 units @ ₹120
```

Sale:

```text
12 units
```

Under FIFO:

```text
10 × ₹100 = ₹1,000
2 × ₹120  = ₹240
COGS = ₹1,240
```

Closing stock:

```text
8 × ₹120 = ₹960
```

## Weighted/Average Concept

Total:

```text
20 units
Total Cost = ₹2,200
Average = ₹110
```

If 12 sold:

```text
Approx. cost = ₹1,320
Closing = ₹880
```

The valuation method can materially affect gross profit and closing stock.

Tally can display or calculate multiple valuation views, but a method available in software is not automatically acceptable for statutory financial statements or tax. Do not change the accounting valuation method casually; document approval, consistency, transition effects, and the applicable reporting rules.

# Appendix X — Loan Accounting

Loans should not be treated as income.

## Loan Received

```text
Bank A/c Dr            500,000
    To Bank Loan A/c           500,000
```

## EMI Payment

Suppose:

```text
EMI = ₹20,000
Principal = ₹15,000
Interest = ₹5,000
```

Entry:

```text
Bank Loan A/c Dr        15,000
Interest Expense A/c Dr  5,000
    To Bank A/c                  20,000
```

Only the interest is normally an expense.

Principal reduces liability.

# Appendix AP — Fraud and Error Red Flags

Watch for:

- Duplicate payments.
- Duplicate supplier masters.
- Round-value journals.
- Weekend/back-dated entries.
- Frequent voucher deletion.
- Negative cash.
- Old suspense.
- Unusual customer credit balances.
- Unusual supplier debit balances.
- Manual tax overrides.
- Payments without invoice.
- Payments to changed bank details.
- Unexplained discounts.
- Inventory adjustments without approval.
- High-value entries just below approval limits.

These are not proof of fraud, but they justify review.

# Final Notes

The most important lesson in Tally is:

> **Do not learn only which key to press. Learn the accounting meaning of the transaction first.**

If you understand:

```text
Business Event
    ↓
Accounting Effect
    ↓
Correct Ledger
    ↓
Correct Voucher
    ↓
Correct Tax/Inventory Treatment
    ↓
Correct Report
```

then you can work confidently not only in Tally.ERP 9, but also in TallyPrime and many other accounting/ERP systems.

A strong Tally user should be able to move from a source document such as an invoice, bank statement, receipt, purchase order or expense bill to the correct accounting entry and then verify its effect on financial statements.
